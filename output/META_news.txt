The chipmaker plans to raise prices starting September 1 to offset industry-wide cost increases, while its outlook for the current quarter came in below analyst expectations JHVEPhoto / Getty Images Qualcomm $QCOM reported fiscal third-quarter net income of $2 billion, or $1.87 per diluted share, on Wednesday, down 25% from $2.67 billion, or $2.43 per diluted share, in the same period a year earlier. Revenue fell 4% to $9.95 billion. Qualcomm attributed the earnings pressure to industrywide cost inflation spanning the chip manufacturing supply chain — from wafer fabrication and assembly through advanced packaging and memory, the company said. Handset chip revenue, Qualcomm's largest segment, dropped 20% year over year to $5.1 billion. Qualcomm stock fell 7.6% in after-hours trading. Qualcomm President and CEO Cristiano Amon said the company plans to raise chip prices across the board beginning September 1 to offset the higher input costs. "Cost went up, prices are going to go up," Amon told CNBC. The company said it expects the pricing changes to benefit gross margins over time as they gradually take effect. Amon also attributed part of the handset weakness to shifting consumer preferences. He pointed to memory price increases as a drag on affordability, saying shoppers are opting for cheaper options within the premium tier or simply holding onto last year's handset, he told CNBC. Automotive stood out as the strongest performer, with sales climbing 61% year over year to $1.59 billion and extending the segment's streak of double-digit annual growth to 23 consecutive quarters, the company said. Internet of Things revenue grew 9% to $1.83 billion. Combined, the two segments grew 28% year over year. Looking ahead to the fiscal fourth quarter, Qualcomm said it anticipates adjusted EPS in the range of $2.05 to $2.25 and revenue between $9.7 billion and $10.5 billion. Analysts had expected adjusted earnings of $2.36 to $2.38 per share on revenue of roughly $10.02 billion to $10.07 billion, according to The Wall Street Journal. The earnings came weeks after Qualcomm outlined an expanded data center AI strategy at its 2026 Investor Day, where it raised its fiscal 2029 non-handset revenue target to $40 billion and announced that Meta $META Platforms would use its new Dragonfly C1000 central processing units. Amon said on Wednesday that non-handset revenue growth is expected to accelerate from 24% in fiscal 2026 to more than 60% in fiscal 2027, the company said. Qualcomm also said Wednesday it had completed its acquisition of Modular Inc., an AI software startup. The deal, valued at $3.92 billion in an all-stock transaction, is intended to strengthen Qualcomm's software capabilities for data center AI inference and deployment. Title: How to Grow on Instagram Without Reels
URL: https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/?p=279347
Time Published: 2026-07-30T10:00:00Z
Full Content:
Social Media Examiner Your Guide to the Marketing Jungle by Michael Stelzner / July 30, 2026 Want to grow on Instagram but dread the idea of filming yourself on camera every day? Wondering if there's a content format that drives real followers and revenue without requiring you to become a full-time video creator? In this article, you'll discover how to use Instagram carousels to build a loyal following, generate leads, and drive sales without ever hitting record on a Reel. The biggest misconception about Instagram growth is that Reels are the only path forward. Katelyn Rhoades built a following of more than 217,000 and a six-figure revenue stream by leaning heavily into educational carousels, and her agency has replicated the approach across multiple niches with consistent results. The advantage comes down to how Instagram's algorithm measures engagement. When someone watches a Reel, they keep scrolling. Carousels demand a different kind of interaction: people stop, read, swipe through slides, go back to earlier slides, save the post, and send it to friends. Instagram measures all of that activity, including how long someone stays on a post, how many slides they swipe through, which slide they leave on, and whether they share it. The algorithm rewards attention. Carousels naturally generate more of it. The benefits extend beyond follower counts. Katelyn's carousel-first strategy has attracted clients, earned features in Forbes, and led to a partnership with Meta. But carousels also solve a practical problem: they eliminate the need to appear on camera. Creators who are stronger at writing or graphic design can lean into those strengths and stay consistent, rather than skipping posts because they don't feel like filming a Reel. Before building a single carousel, though, one foundational question needs an answer: What's the goal? Growing a following, making sales, and inspiring an audience each require different approaches. That single objective shapes everything from the hook to the call to action. The most common mistake creators make with carousels is starting with visuals and trying to force a story around them. Katelyn recommends the opposite order: start with the narrative, then find or create images to support it. Identify one specific person to write for, not a general audience. Katelyn suggests picturing a single dream client and asking: What are they struggling with? What question are they asking? What transformation do they need? When content speaks to one person's situation, everyone who relates feels like it was written directly for them. From there, the carousel needs a clear arc. The first slide should hit the viewer's pain point hard enough that they feel personally called out. The middle slides deliver value, and the final slide tells them what to do next. But a critical balance governs the middle: the content should be useful but incomplete. The reader walks away having learned something real, but not so much that they feel they no longer need the creator's help. This is the same principle behind effective webinars, and it's what separates carousels that build authority from carousels that actually drive business. Instagram allows up to 20 slides per carousel, but more isn't better. Katelyn's agency typically produces 8 to 10 slides. Every slide should earn the next swipe. If a slide doesn't move the story forward or strengthen the point, it gets cut. For creators who aren't natural storytellers, AI can help with the storyboarding. Even bullet-pointing the core idea and feeding it into ChatGPT produces a workable narrative framework. Katelyn offers a free plug-and-play ChatGPT prompt designed specifically for organizing carousel stories; find it in her bio, below. The text hook is the most important element of a carousel after the visual. If the first slide doesn't stop someone’s scroll, the rest of the content never gets seen. The AI Business Society is your trusted guide. Get expert-led training you can put to work immediately. Plus a community of marketers sharing what's actually working. Two questions should come before writing a single word: Who is this for, and what should they feel after reading it? Negative hooks consistently outperform positive ones. People naturally pay more attention to mistakes they might be making, myths they've believed, or things they're doing wrong. “How to grow on Instagram today” is easy to scroll past. “You are killing your Instagram reach if you don't fix this one thing” creates immediate curiosity because readers wonder whether they're making the mistake. Similarly, “5 ways to get more clients” becomes “This one thing is what's keeping people from buying from you.” Readability matters as much as the words. Katelyn recommends clean, simple fonts, big text, and plenty of white space. If the text is hard on people's eyes, they'll keep scrolling regardless of how compelling the words are. Black or white text provides the highest contrast, though yellow text has been performing unusually well recently, likely because it breaks the pattern most creators default to. One critical rule: the hook must create a curiosity gap without resolving it. If the first slide answers the promise, there's no reason for the viewer to keep swiping. Katelyn sees this mistake constantly. The hook's only job is to stop the scroll. Everything after that delivers on the promise. Katelyn uses ChatGPT to refine hooks through multiple iterations, asking it to “make it spicier,” “make the call-out stronger,” or “make it more Gen Z coded.” That last prompt tends to produce attention-grabbing language like “diabolical” or “completely unhinged” that resonates on the platform. Pro Tip: Instagram recently introduced a native text overlay feature that lets creators add text directly on top of photos within the app, without needing an external design tool. The final slide is where most creators miss opportunities. After working to stop the scroll, hook the reader, and deliver valuable content, many carousels end without telling the viewer what to do next. These three types of calls to action serve different goals: General Comments Improve Reach: Ask viewers to comment by answering a question or responding to a prompt. Each resulting comment signals to the algorithm that the content is worth distributing. More comments mean more reach. Shares Leverage Engagement: Shares have become the most important engagement metric on Instagram, surpassing saves, which dominated the previous year. “Share this with someone who needs to hear it” or “Tag your best friend in the comments” delivers more algorithmic value than asking for a save. Sales CTAs and DM Automation Drive Revenue: This is the approach that turned Katelyn's carousels into a direct revenue channel. Tell viewers to comment with a specific keyword, such as “starter kit,” and use a DM automation tool like ManyChat or Stan Store to automatically send anyone who comments that keyword a message with a link to your offer, freebie, or sales page. For creators with smaller followings or those not ready to invest in automation tools, the same keyword approach works with manual replies. Ask viewers to comment with a keyword, then personally DM each commenter with the relevant link. The engagement benefits are the same; only the delivery method changes. With the story planned and the hook written, the next step is building the visual presentation. The choices here break into three decisions: which format to use, what imagery to use, and whether to animate. Instagram offers two carousel dimensions: square and rectangle. The rectangle format takes up more real estate in the feed, which increases the chances of stopping the scroll. Authenticity outperforms polish. After posting thousands of pieces of content across client accounts across multiple niches, Katelyn's agency has consistently seen camera-roll photos outperform professional brand photography. Mirror selfies, behind-the-scenes moments, casual snapshots at restaurants, and candid daily-life images all work well. The key is to lead with a face on the first slide. A human face creates an immediate connection that stops the scroll. The remaining slides can include anything that supports the story, such as event photos, food shots, candid moments, or even template-based designs. Creators who don't want to use personal photos can work entirely with templates. Canva and Adobe Express both offer pre-built carousel templates that can be customized with brand colors and text. Katelyn notes that Canva's default templates may produce middling results, but they're a solid starting point. AI-generated images can also work when paired with a strong story, though the critical factor is that visuals feel intentional and on-brand rather than generic. Building a library of casual photos makes carousel creation faster over time. Katelyn recommends documenting daily life with photos constantly, even when there's no immediate plan for them. The iPhone's photo search feature then makes retrieval fast: searching keywords like “house,” “drink,” or “restaurant” pulls up matching photos without scrolling through thousands of images. Pro Tip: Instagram recently introduced a native text overlay feature that lets creators add text directly on top of photo dumps within the app, without needing an external design tool. Animated carousels consistently outperform static ones in Katelyn's experience, but the animation only needs to appear on the first slide. That single animated slide creates a pattern interrupt, where the extra second of attention can be the difference between a scroll past and a new follower. Animating every slide hasn't shown a meaningful performance benefit, and each animated slide is downloaded as a separate MP4 file, slowing production. There are two simple approaches to animation in Canva. The first is dropping a stock video clip onto the slide as a background: click “Elements,” then “Video,” search for a keyword like “train” or “clouds,” and layer the hook text on top. The second is animating the text itself using Canva's built-in options like “wiggle” or “typewriter,” which add motion without requiring any video. Google's Gemini can animate static images with a simple “animate this” prompt, adding subtle zooms or environmental motion like steam rising from a coffee cup. And if a photo doesn't leave enough headroom for the hook text, Photoshop's generative fill can extend the canvas by analyzing the image and generating photorealistic filler content above the subject's head. The split followed two days of sharp volatility in which the Nikkei was driven largely by swings in artificial intelligence, semiconductor and technology-related shares. The Nikkei’s rise was modest but important after the index fell heavily on July 28 and July 29. Investors remained cautious, but the market avoided a third straight decline as bargain buying returned to some electronics names and as selling in the most volatile AI shares appeared to slow. Nikkei CNBC’s market framing centered on a fragile technical rebound rather than a decisive recovery. The market remained heavily influenced by overseas semiconductor positioning, South Korean chip shares and U.S. technology earnings, but investors were also watching whether Tokyo could find support ahead of the BOJ’s July 30-31 meeting. Electronics shares provided the main support. Hitachi rose 3.3%, while Kokusai Electric gained 4.7%, helping lift the Nikkei despite weakness elsewhere. The buying suggested that investors were still willing to hold companies linked to data centers, power infrastructure, advanced equipment and industrial digitalization, even after the recent AI-led selloff. The rebound was selective. Investors continued to distinguish between companies with strong earnings visibility and those exposed to crowded positioning, margin-financed trading or uncertainty over the return on AI investment. The market’s willingness to buy electronics names showed that the AI theme has not disappeared, but the past two weeks have made investors more demanding about valuations, guidance and cash-flow discipline. Financial shares weighed on the broader market. Nomura Holdings fell 6.7%, while Mizuho Financial Group declined 2.8%, pulling down the TOPIX despite the Nikkei’s advance. The selling reflected profit-taking and caution ahead of the BOJ decision after banks had benefited from expectations of higher interest rates. The fall in financial shares also showed the difficulty of trading the BOJ theme. Higher rates can support bank lending margins, but rapid increases in yields can also damage bond portfolios, raise funding costs and create concern about the wider economy. Investors were reluctant to extend recent gains in banks before hearing Governor Kazuo Ueda’s assessment of inflation, the yen and future rate increases. The yen remained near its weakest level in about 40 years. The dollar traded around 163.26 yen, compared with 163.55 yen at the previous Tokyo stock-market close. The slight recovery in the yen did little to ease concern that currency weakness is becoming a major inflation risk for households and companies. The weak yen remains a double-edged factor for Japanese equities. Exporters benefit when overseas earnings are converted into yen, but the currency also raises the cost of imported oil, liquefied natural gas, food, chemicals, industrial components and consumer goods. That pressure is increasingly important because households are already facing higher prices for daily necessities. A weak yen, even with oil prices below their recent highs, can keep gasoline, electricity, food distribution and imported goods expensive in yen terms. Japanese government bond yields remained under close watch as the BOJ began its two-day policy meeting. The central bank raised its policy rate to 1% in June and is widely expected to leave rates unchanged this week, but investors are looking for any signal that another increase remains likely later in 2026. Reuters reported that the BOJ is expected to maintain a hawkish tone and leave room for further rate hikes as inflation pressure builds from the weak yen, energy costs and robust global demand related to AI investment. The central bank is also expected to remain ambiguous about timing while it waits to see how much producer-price pressure passes through to consumers. The BOJ’s quarterly outlook report will be central to the market reaction. Reuters reported that the board is likely to revise up its fiscal 2026 growth forecast because fears of a severe Middle East hit have receded, while slightly lowering its inflation forecast because of subsidies and lower oil prices compared with earlier assumptions. Even so, the BOJ is expected to keep warning that inflation could exceed its 2% target. Corporate inflation expectations are elevated, food and daily-necessity price increases continue, and the weak yen keeps import costs high. Governor Ueda faces a difficult communication challenge. A message that is too soft could increase pressure on the yen and raise doubts about the BOJ’s inflation-fighting credibility. A message that is too hawkish could push bond yields higher, unsettle equities and increase tension with a government focused on growth and fiscal support. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s administration remains under scrutiny from bond and currency markets. Its economic strategy calls for more than 370 trillion yen in combined public and private investment through fiscal 2040, targeting semiconductors, artificial intelligence, energy security, defense, shipbuilding, robotics, space and other strategic industries. Title: Flirting with $5 trillion, Apple has never been more valuable—or a riskier bet for investors
URL: https://fortune.com/2026/07/30/is-apple-stock-a-buy-valuation-outlook-earnings-market-cap-5-trillion/
Time Published: 2026-07-30T07:00:00Z
Full Content:
Around midday on July 28, Apple stock jumped around 3% to achieve, for the first time, a market cap of $5 trillion, hitting a number that only one enterprise, Nvidia, had ever reached. A few days before, in fact, Apple had surged past the GPU giant to regain its mantle as the world’s most valuable company (Nvidia has recently retreated to $4.79 trillion). It looks as if Apple got its most recent boost from a Bloomberg report that the iPhone maker is planning a big push into the smart-home sector by launching a new Siri-powered home hub and updated Apple TV, alongside a fresh HomePod mini, all of which could potentially hit the market by this fall. Plus, Apple used to get brickbats from Wall Street for its super-cautious approach in AI investment. Now that it appears the hyperscalers may be overbuilding data centers en route to a glut, the analysts and money managers are applauding Apple’s restraint. Still, the recent moonshot in Apple’s stock poses a tough choice for folks and funds mulling a purchase right now, or tempted to sell and profit from the recent windfall on fears it will fade: Apple has just become something it seldom was before, extremely, even scarily, expensive. The recent liftoff makes Apple by far the priciest member of the Magnificent Seven, of course excluding Tesla. Its price/earnings ratio reached 41.2 on the $5 trillion day, between 37% and 145% above the figures for Nvidia (30.2), Amazon (27.7), Meta (21.6), and Alphabet (16.8). Historically, Apple has handed shareholders significantly more cents for every dollar in earnings than it provides today. From 2013 to 2020, its P/E never exceeded 20, and averaged around 16. Even post-COVID, its multiple fluctuated around a median of 28, and as recently as the close of Q1 2024, sat at 26.4. In fact, from early 2022 to Q1 of 2024, Apple’s EPS flatlined, and so did its share price, leaving its P/E well below 30. Profits barely budged until mid-2025, then accelerated, rising 25% on a trailing, four-quarter basis through Q1 of 2026. But in that span, its stock broke free, doubling from $170 in Q1 of 2024 to today’s $350. That huge divergence where the shares waxed at four times the rate of profits drove its P/E to the current, vertiginous 41-plus. Apple depends heavily on share buybacks to raise its EPS. In fiscal 2024 and 2025 (ended Sept. 30) it spent $185 billion in repurchases, equal to 92% of its GAAP net earnings. When its P/E was 25, Apple was raising EPS four cents, or 4%, for every dollar in buybacks. But at a 41 multiple, it’s lifting per share profits by much less, by 2.4 cents and a like percentage. Hence, investors face two headwinds. First, they’ll get much less lift from buybacks than in the past, a puny one in fact for the simple reason that Apple will be repurchasing not cheap but ultra-costly shares. Second, it’s highly unlikely that Apple’s P/E will remain at 41. That’s almost 50% over the S&P 500’s already elevated average, and just look at where its Mag Seven confreres are sitting. If its multiple drifts back to 30 over the next five years, Apple will need a 5% annual push from a combination of repurchases and earnings expansion just to stay even, to maintain its share price at current levels. Put simply, at these prices, Apple must find a big new growth engine to drive its stock price higher. Apple’s business is doing fine, but its stock is doing stupendously. That big split is the rub that makes Apple a much less shiny investment.
