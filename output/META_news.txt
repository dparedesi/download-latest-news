Full Content:
Getty Apple's market capitalisation briefly surpassed $5 trillion for the first time yesterday, making it only the second company ever to achieve that milestone after Nvidia. At a session high of $342.89 on Wall Street last night, Apple's market value stood at $5.036 trillion. Apple became the most valuable company in the world earlier this month, overtaking chipmaker Nvidia - which had been at the top since June 2025 and was the first company ever to breach the $5 trillion threshold. Nvidia's shares were last up 0.53% at $197.63, valuing it at $4.78 trillion. Apple has benefited from strong demand for its products as well as its decision to skip the ongoing AI spending race among Big Tech rivals that is sapping their cash flows and saddling them with humongous debt. Apple's stock has gained about 25% so far this year, outperforming other so-called "Magnificent 7" technology companies including Nvidia, Meta, Alphabet and Microsoft. The iPhone maker has relied on Google's AI technology to power new services such as a revamped Siri, avoiding the hefty infrastructure costs associated with surging data-center investments. Apple's decision to hold iPhone prices steady last month when it unveiled increases for MacBooks and iPads has also bolstered demand as buyers scooped up the company's flagship device ahead of expected price hikes later this year, analysts have said. The company launched a device leasing programme this week in the US through payments firm Klarna. "Apple has resisted the AI spending race, betting that customer experience - not infrastructure investment - will ultimately determine the winners," said Dipanjan Chatterjee, vice president and principal analyst at Forrester. "The new leasing programme is a clever response: it doesn't reduce the price of an iPhone, but it changes how consumers perceive the cost by replacing sticker shock with a predictable monthly payment." Apple is set to report its third-quarter earnings after the market close on Thursday, with analysts expecting a more than 15% jump in quarterly revenue from a year earlier. The company plans to follow up the Rubin Ultra with a chip series called Feynman in 2028. It will be the first Nvidia graphics card lineup to feature die stacking, which means that its chiplets will be placed atop one another in a three-dimensional configuration. That’s a more than tenfold increase over Nvidia’s Vera Rubin appliances. The density boost will be made possible by the introduction of switches with co-packaged optics technology. CPO hardware makes it possible to link the GPUs in a rack without chips called transceivers that are usually needed for the task. That lowers hardware costs and power use.
