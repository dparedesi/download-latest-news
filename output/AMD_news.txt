Core Scientific reported strong second-quarter revenue growth as AI colocation became its largest business, though a non-cash accounting charge resulted in a $1.15 billion net loss. Digital infrastructure company Core Scientific more than doubled its second-quarter revenue as rapid growth in its artificial intelligence and high-performance computing (HPC) colocation business continued to reshape its earnings profile following its pivot beyond Bitcoin mining. The company reported Tuesday that Q2 revenue increased to $164.2 million, up from $78.6 million a year earlier. Colocation revenue accounted for $136.7 million of the total, compared with just $10.6 million in the same period last year, while gross profit increased to $70 million from $5 million. Despite the revenue surge, Core Scientific reported a net loss of $1.15 billion, driven primarily by a non-cash accounting charge related to the rising value of outstanding warrants as its share price increased. The results underscore how several Bitcoin mining companies have diversified into AI and HPC infrastructure, seeking more stable, long-term revenue streams as demand for data center capacity surges. Once one of the world’s largest publicly traded Bitcoin miners, Core Scientific now generates the bulk of its revenue from colocation services while maintaining a comparatively modest Bitcoin treasury of fewer than 1,000 BTC, according to industry data. Core Scientific shares fell more than 4% following the earnings release, trimming its year-to-date gains. Core Scientific (CORZ) stock is up 36% this year. It is initially anchored by 15-year agreements covering 530 megawatts across several US sites beginning in 2027, with the potential to expand over time. When a company tells you it expects earnings per share to jump more than fifteen percent from one quarter to the next, you listen. The question for anyone holding or watching JCI is simple: What is the market waiting to see before it believes the hype? That Record Backlog Needs to Start Converting On paper, the bull case looks formidable. Fueled by demand from data centers, the company is sitting on a backlog that grew 26% to a record $20 billion. That should provide a clear runway for growth. Yet beneath that headline number, there’s a catch that helps explain the market’s patience. Management noted that while they expect about 70% of that backlog to convert to revenue in the next year, the rest is “a little bit challenged right now,” partly due to customer-side delays in securing things like power infrastructure. A record backlog is only as good as the revenue it generates. The market seems to be withholding its applause until it sees that massive order book reliably turn into cash flow, proving the growth will translate from paper promises to actual profits. Why Sluggish Service Orders Are a Red Flag The other piece of the puzzle is the part of the business that’s supposed to be steady and predictable. Service contracts make up about a third of the company’s revenue, but management acknowledged on their call that orders in this segment were “a little softer,” with particular weakness in the security business. While the company is rebalancing price and volume there, any softness in a recurring revenue stream can spook investors. It creates a tension: are the massive, one-time project wins from data centers enough to offset signs of sluggishness in the core, repeatable service business? The market appears to be saying, “Show me.” How Bumpy Could the Ride Get From Here? This quiet standoff may not last. The options market is pricing in an unusually large expected move around the company’s next earnings report. JCI’s implied volatility currently sits at 32%, which is in the 80th percentile of its range over the past year. In plain, the options market is betting that the stock is poised for a significant swing, breaking out of its recent calm. The company has raised the bar. Now it has to clear it, and the market is done waiting on the sidelines. Management has made its bet with a higher forecast; the market is waiting to see the cards.
