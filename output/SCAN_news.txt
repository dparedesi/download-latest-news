Historical trends also suggest that the start of the monsoon does not necessarily determine the final outcome. Although June 2026 was among India’s driest in more than a century, the eventual impact on the economy will depend largely on how rainfall progresses through July, August and September. In 1926, for instance, the monsoon experienced one of its weakest starts on record before staging a strong recovery between July and September. The season eventually ended with rainfall 11% above normal. The analysis indicates that consecutive years of deficient rainfall generally have a more significant impact on farm incomes, rural consumption and economic growth than a single delayed monsoon.
