In-Language News Contact Us 888-776-0942 from 8 AM - 10 PM ET Jul 30, 2026, 07:30 ET Share this article Future Union's All-World Defense 300 evaluates firms by solutions, solving the most critical challenges facing allied governments — mapping strengths of the alliance and gaps WASHINGTON, July 30, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Today, Future Union, a post-partisan organization dedicated to mobilizing private capital and enterprise released the All-World Defense 300 and its companion index, the Allied Defense 100. The lists highlight the private companies at the forefront of international defense innovation. The All-World Defense 300, a data-driven assessment of private sector companies, analyzes the defense technology landscape and evaluates, scores, and ranks companies that have proven critical technological capabilities. The Allied Defense 100 includes the defense tech innovators setting new standards that are still scaling. Together the lists constitute the first global ranking of the private companies underwriting the growing defense tech sector based on demonstrated, fielded capability rather than fundraising, revenue or public market capitalization. Global consulting firm Ankura Consulting, global law firm Ashurst Perkins Coie, and Silicon Valley Bank, a division of First Citizens Bank, contributed to this effort. "The All-World Defense 300 examines real, deployed capabilities across the United States and our allies to provide the global defense industrial base with actionable intelligence to identify gaps and areas of potential oversaturation," said Andrew King, founder of Future Union. "Due to ongoing conflicts around the world, for the first time we are seeing a significant shift in capabilities from the U.S. to its allies, especially in strike capability, with allied companies now outnumbering their American counterparts, 22 to 17. The Allied Defense 100, meanwhile, examines innovations on the verge of scale; emerging companies that have tangible solutions which could place them in the All-World Defense 300 in the future." The United States retains command of the foundational tiers of allied power, holding 100 percent of the top production ("Arsenal") tier in both advanced compute and strategic resources, and 79 percent of the Arsenal tier across all eight verticals. "The U.S. government has committed billions of dollars to support the next generation of defense, including mission-critical systems that are essential to safeguarding national interests and make the U.S. Department of War more operationally efficient," said Jim Brenner, partner at Ashurst Perkins Coie. "The All-World Defense 300 are shaping the future of defense, they are the companies that will realize innovations such as the Golden Dome, Replicator, Agentic AI, and the next challenge we have not yet imagined." The lists were developed by Future Union using primary source data to include FAR-based contracts, OTAs, SBIR/STTR awards, DPA Title III funding, and global procurement announcements, which were then combined through a scoring system weighted by government-contract demand (55%), independent comparable capabilities (30%), and resource availability (15%). Demonstrable success and capabilities were taken into account, and each vertical is segmented into three categories: Arsenal (foundational and fielded at scale), Forge (scaling and magnified importance), and Spark (innovation and notable for its current potential). "This represents one of the first truly global views of where the United States and its allies stand, and where further capability development is needed," said Frank Edwards, Senior Managing Director at Ankura. "The All-World Defense 300 highlights how rapidly commercial innovation is shaping modern defense and reinforces the importance of collaboration across companies and nations." Of the 300 companies, 92 are headquartered outside the United States, spanning 26 nations. The international base has matured into an increasingly capable "Sovereign Stack," concentrated in distinct regional centers: the United Kingdom in deep technology, Israel in cyber-kinetic systems, Ukraine in combat-proven autonomy, and an ascendant Turkey in uncrewed systems and armor. However, more than mere defense tech, the list highlights the often overlooked, "Shadow Primes" and industrial companies that are essential to supply chains, systems and tech-enabled advances, the absence of which would render inoperable most technology-centric production. "We work with founders across the defense sector, to help them achieve their business growth goals," stated Dax Williamson, Managing Director and head of the Aerospace, Defense & Security Innovation Group at Silicon Valley Bank, a Division of First Citizens Bank. "The All-World Defense 300 and the Allied Defense 100 indices highlight the founders and companies across the defense sector that have significant opportunities ahead and we aim to support growing companies as they develop." Both indices will be published annually. Future Union also announced a weekly leaderboard tracking defense contract awards across the United States and its allies, updated every Monday. The aim is to bring public competition to a procurement process that has long operated in the shadows — and to press allied governments toward disclosure standards that make real scorekeeping possible. Both lists are available at FutureUnion.co. Title: Meet the 2026 Chicago Class of Next City Vanguards
URL: https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/meet-the-2026-chicago-class-of-next-city-vanguards
Time Published: 2026-07-28T10:00:00Z
Full Content:
More than 40 changemakers from across the country will gather in September. Deonna Anderson July 28, 2026 (Photo by Johannes Krupinski) This is your first of three free stories this month. Become a free or sustaining member to read unlimited articles, webinars and ebooks. Next City today announces the class of 41 Vanguards who will convene in Chicago from September 22 through 25. This is Next City’s 18th Vanguard conference, an annual experiential gathering of rising urban leaders who work to improve cities across sectors. The 2026 Vanguard fellows work in sectors including community organizing, urban planning, policy, economic development, and the arts. They hold positions at influential organizations, including the New York City Department of Transportation, Stanford University, and National Community Investment Fund. Vanguards from Chicago are working with Community Desk Chicago, UnidosUS, Cooperation Racine, the Midwest Environmental Justice Network, and other organizations. View the entire Vanguard cohort in Next City’s directory of new members and alumni, now numbering more than 700 city-builders. Programming for this year’s convening has been developed by the Next City team in close partnership with a Chicago-based host committee. It was guided by the theme: “The America that Never Was: In the Pursuit of….” Throughout the convening, we’ll explore the gap between what America has promised for centuries and what communities — particularly Black and Brown ones — have received. We’ll also highlight the ways that communities across Chicago have built models for building community wealth, despite decades of disinvestment. “Chicago is a city that offers a lot of lessons about what it means to build community power and wealth after decades of disinvestment,” said Next City Editorial Director Deonna Anderson. “I’m looking forward to sharing and learning more about the city’s South, West and North Sides with this newly minted Vanguard cohort.” Next City, a nonprofit news outlet, launched Vanguard in 2009, aligning with its core value of fostering connections between changemakers. The conference supports its mission to unleash the transformative power of solutions-based journalism, equipping communities and their leaders to reimagine cities as liberated places of economic, environmental, and racial justice. As does all of Next City’s work, the event programming uplifts diverse perspectives and marginalized narratives. The conference, which is supported by a group of generous sponsors and grantmakers, will unfold as a four-day series of presentations, workshops, and neighborhood tours. The program doesn’t stop there, however; the lessons learned serve as a launchpad for a year-long reporting project focused on housing, in collaboration with two local newsrooms. That work is made possible with support from Press Forward Chicago. Now meet this year’s Vanguard cohort (in alphabetical order by last name). Alicia Olushola Ajayi is a designer, strategist, and writer whose Black placemaking practice uncovers and amplifies stories as acts of resistance and joy. Rachel is City Bureau’s Chicago Director of Documenters and a proud Chicago native. They blend pastoral leadership, program management, policy advocacy, and community organizing to bring a fresh perspective on civic engagement. Committed to justice, equity, and transparency, Rachel helps equip residents to participate in local government and civic life. Previously at LISC, she has a background in placemaking, economic development, and cultural strategy. She holds degrees from the University of Pennsylvania and the University of Illinois. Serrater Chapman is Director of Applied Research at the Woodstock Institute, where she leads research on lending disparities, consumer financial protections, and equitable access to capital. A lifelong Chicago south sider, she uses data-driven research and community-centered policy strategies to advance financial justice, housing equity, and inclusive economic development.
