Title: I’m a diehard Android user, but I still envy these 7 iOS features
URL: https://www.androidauthority.com/diehard-android-user-ios-features-i-want-3691074/
Time Published: 2026-08-03T10:00:05Z
Full Content:
Affiliate links on Android Authority may earn us a commission. Learn more. Aug 3, 2026 — 6:00 AM ET I’ve been an Android user for as long as I can remember, and I have no plans to give up my Galaxy S26 for any iPhone — not even if Apple makes it fold in half someday. But that doesn’t mean I’m blind to what the other side does better. I keep an iPhone around for work, and while I can’t imagine using it as my daily driver, there are a few iOS features that genuinely make me jealous. Some of them Android doesn’t have at all. Others exist but feel half-baked by comparison. Either way, they sting a little. Google Messages has come a long way with RCS support, end-to-end encryption, typing indicators, and reactions. But all of this only works when everyone’s on board, and that includes your carrier and even the messaging app you use. iMessage just works, even without a SIM card. The gap isn’t just about reliability, though. iMessage is genuinely better in terms of features, too. We’re talking about better group chats, superior media sharing, SharePlay, Memoji, and fun effects. And let’s not forget FaceTime. Anyone who’s used it knows just how good it is compared to whatever video call solution Android offers. All of this gets even harder to argue against when you factor in Apple’s ecosystem. You can switch from your iPhone to an iPad or a Mac, and your messages just follow you. RCS can replicate some of this on Android, sure, but it’s nowhere near seamless. Live activities on iPhone are another underrated gem. The idea is simple. It pins real-time, glanceable updates to your lock screen or the Dynamic Island. You can check how far your Uber is, what time your food will arrive, your favorite team’s score, or if your flight is boarding — all without opening apps. It works for timers, music players, workouts, and a lot more. I know some Android phones have tried to replicate this, and you do get Live Updates in the notifications panel, but Apple’s implementation is in a different class. I personally love how it lets you switch between multiple activities and even dismiss them with a swipe on the Dynamic Island. It’s a genius way to make that giant pill more useful than it is an eyesore. StandBy mode turns your iPhone into a smart display the moment you set it down horizontally while it’s charging. It shows widgets, clocks, photos, news, stocks, calendars, and even Live Activities. At night, it dims to a red-tinted display so it doesn’t blind you. Honestly, it felt gimmicky when I first tried it. But then I started to miss it on my Android phone. And that’s usually the tell. Yes, Android has always-on display support, but it doesn’t scratch the same itch. The widget options are limited, it doesn’t use the entire screen, and it doesn’t feel fun. Apple’s StandBy mode actually makes you want to look at your phone while it’s charging. Apple’s Shortcuts app makes it ridiculously easy to automate certain tasks on iPhone. You can build automations for all kinds of things, like turning text into audio, starting a pomodoro timer, shortening URLs, creating GIFs, and a lot more. A few of these come ready-made, but you can also start from scratch and get as creative as you want. Android doesn’t have anything similar. Pixel phones have Routines, but they’re pretty barebones by comparison. Samsung’s Modes and Routines are more capable, but they don’t work that well with third-party apps. The only viable options are apps like Tasker and MacroDroid, but neither is entirely free and, more importantly, comes from third-party developers. Swiping down anywhere on the iPhone home screen opens Spotlight, and what you can do from that single search bar is impressive. You can use it to find messages, look up contacts, run a unit conversion, download apps, check a stock price, set alarms, and even do web searches — all without opening a single app. Android’s search bar isn’t as capable, at least not on all phones. Pixel’s search can surface app content and settings, and Samsung has its own device search that’s somewhat similar to Spotlight. Still, there’s nothing that’s available across all Android phones, which is sad. If I could get Google to only copy one thing from iOS, it’d be this. iCloud backups are thorough and leave almost nothing behind. Apps, contacts, settings, messages, photos, videos, passwords, health data — it covers everything. So even if you lose your iPhone, your precious data is always safe. And of course, upgrading to a new one is also hassle-free. All you really need to do is sign in to your iCloud account, and everything becomes available in a few hours. There’s no need to keep both phones plugged in with a cable and wait. Android’s backup situation is fragmented in a way that’s hard to defend. Google One covers app data, call history, contacts, messages, and a few other things, but it’s not thorough. Transfers work reasonably well when you stay within the same ecosystem: Pixel to Pixel or Galaxy to Galaxy. But the moment you cross over to a phone running a different skin, you’re looking at missing settings or broken app data. This one might ruffle some feathers in the Android community, but hear me out. iPhone has a few privacy features that I genuinely miss on Android. App Tracking Transparency is a good example. Any app that wants to track you across other apps and websites has to ask permission first, and you can say no. You know it’s working when Meta publicly complains about it costing them billions in ad revenue. Then there’s Hide My Email, which lets you generate random email aliases when signing up for apps and websites, so you can keep your actual email free from spam. Heck, Android doesn’t even have a native app locking yet. And no, Private Space and Secure Folder don’t count. To be fair, some of these are in the works, but as of now, Android lags behind iOS in terms of privacy features. Nothing on this list is enough to make me switch, but that also kind of makes it worse. Is there any cool iOS feature you feel Android lacks? Drop it in the comments. Elon Musk gets plenty of attention. He’ll get even more this week. SpaceX is set to issue its first quarterly earnings report as a public company Tuesday afternoon, calling attention to Silicon Valley’s surging investment in AI and the supply chain supporting the buildout. The results will follow a volatile seven weeks for Elon Musk’s newly public moonshot. The rocket, connectivity and AI company sold shares for $135 in the largest initial public offering on record in June. The stock shot up in its first days, surpassing $225, and then steadily slid, falling below its IPO price and touching a low of $107 last week. Enthusiasm for SpaceX stock has flagged amid operational setbacks and a pullback in AI stocks. Tech giants have issued new stock and used debt to finance their increasingly expensive AI data center buildout, raising questions about when the investments will pay off. Investors recently dumped shares of several Magnificent 7 members, including Musk’s EV maker Tesla, after the companies indicated their AI spending spree is far from over. SpaceX and its counterparts’ big AI spending has been a tailwind for chip, memory and data storage stocks, which have soared this year as builders snapped up their products. Suppliers, including Advanced Micro Devices, Western Digital and SanDisk, will report this week, along with energy companies benefiting from the AI boom. This week also offers earnings from outside Silicon Valley. A number of consumer companies report, including the streaming service Spotify and media giants Paramount Skydance and Walt Disney. Results are also expected from McDonald’s, Wendy’s and the parent company of Burger King and Popeyes. The Dow had its worst day in 15 months on Wednesday as the Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged. Stocks started to regain ground Thursday, when data showed the Fed’s preferred inflation index cooled in June. Mega-cap tech stocks added to the turbulence throughout the week with mixed earnings reports.
